Divorce & Separation Law in Scotland

Many people use the terms separation and divorce interchangeably, but they are different legal processes.

Separation occurs when spouses decide to live apart and bring their relationship to an end. During separation, important decisions often need to be made regarding:

  • Family finances
  • The matrimonial home
  • Savings and investments
  • Pensions
  • Business interests
  • Child arrangements
  • Ongoing financial support

Divorce is the legal process that formally ends a marriage.

Taking advice at an early stage can help avoid disputes, clarify your legal position and place you in a stronger position when negotiating a settlement.

A Step-by-Step Guide to Divorce in Scotland

Many people contact us unsure where to start. The process can feel overwhelming, particularly when children, property or finances are involved. The following guide explains the key stages.

1
Step 1

Obtain Legal Advice

Early legal advice helps you understand:

  • Your rights
  • Your financial position
  • The likely timescale
  • Available dispute resolution options
  • Potential outcomes

Every family situation is different, so personalised advice is important.

2
Step 2

Identify the Grounds for Divorce

Scottish law requires proof that the marriage has broken down irretrievably. This can usually be established through:

  • One year of separation where both parties consent to divorce
  • Two years of separation where one party does not consent
  • Unreasonable behaviour

We can advise on the most appropriate basis for your circumstances.

3
Step 3

Resolve Financial Matters

Financial issues are often the most significant aspect of divorce. This may involve:

  • The family home
  • Savings
  • Investments
  • Pensions
  • Business interests
  • Debts
  • Other matrimonial assets

Scottish family law seeks a fair division of matrimonial property. Determining what is fair depends on the specific circumstances of each case.

4
Step 4

Agree Arrangements for Children

If children are involved, decisions may need to be made regarding:

  • Where the children will live
  • Contact arrangements
  • Schooling
  • Holidays
  • Important welfare decisions

The welfare of the child remains the court's paramount consideration.

5
Step 5

Finalise the Divorce

Once matters have been resolved, the divorce can proceed through either:

Simplified Divorce Procedure

This may be available where:

  • The parties meet the legal separation requirements
  • There are no children under 16
  • Financial matters have been resolved

Ordinary Divorce Procedure

This process may be required where:

  • Children under 16 are involved
  • Financial issues remain unresolved
  • The divorce is disputed

Our family law team can advise which procedure applies to your circumstances.

Financial Settlements in Scottish Divorce Cases

One of the most common concerns during divorce is financial security. Scottish courts generally focus on the value of matrimonial property acquired during the marriage.

Issues commonly addressed include:

The Family Home

The family home is often the most valuable asset. Various options may be available, including:

  • Selling the property
  • Transferring ownership
  • Delaying sale until a later date

Pensions

Pensions can represent a substantial asset and are frequently included within divorce settlements.

Businesses and Professional Practices

Business owners often require specialist advice. Denise Hooper has extensive experience dealing with complex financial cases involving company interests and overseas assets.

Debt

Debts accumulated during the marriage may form part of the overall financial settlement and require careful consideration.

Separation Agreements

Many couples choose to formalise arrangements before divorce through a Separation Agreement.

A Separation Agreement can record arrangements relating to:

  • Property
  • Finances
  • Child arrangements
  • Ongoing financial support

Having a properly drafted agreement can provide certainty and reduce the risk of future disputes.

Child Arrangements After Separation

Parents often worry about how separation will affect their children. Scottish law focuses on what is in the child's best interests.

Factors that may be considered include:

  • The child's views
  • Their age and maturity
  • Educational needs
  • Emotional wellbeing
  • Existing family relationships

Our family law team works with parents to find practical arrangements that place children first and minimise conflict wherever possible.

Alternatives to Court Proceedings

Not every family dispute requires court action. At Wright & Crawford, we actively explore solutions that can reduce stress, legal costs and delays.

Family Mediation

Mediation involves a neutral professional helping both parties reach an agreement. As an accredited family mediator, Denise Hooper can assist families in resolving disputes constructively.

Collaborative Family Law

Collaborative law allows couples and their solicitors to work together towards a negotiated settlement without court proceedings.

Arbitration

Family arbitration offers a private alternative to court. An independent arbitrator makes a binding decision on disputed issues. This process is often faster and more flexible than litigation.

Negotiated Settlements

Many cases are resolved through direct negotiation between solicitors, avoiding the need for formal court action.

Common Questions About Divorce and Separation in Scotland

How long does a divorce take in Scotland?

Timescales vary depending on finances, children and whether the divorce is contested.

Do I need to go to court to get divorced?

Not always. Many cases are resolved without a court hearing.

What happens to the family home?

The outcome depends on ownership, finances and the wider settlement.

Can we reach an agreement without litigation?

Yes. Mediation, collaborative law and negotiation can often resolve disputes.

When should I speak to a solicitor?

As early as possible. Early advice can help protect your position and avoid costly mistakes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get divorced after one year of separation?

Yes, if both spouses agree to the divorce.

What if my spouse refuses to divorce?

You can usually apply after two years of separation.

Is mediation required before divorce?

No, but it can help resolve disputes without court action.

Are pensions included in divorce settlements?

Yes, pensions may form part of matrimonial property.

Do I need a solicitor for a divorce?

Not legally, but legal advice can protect your rights and finances.
